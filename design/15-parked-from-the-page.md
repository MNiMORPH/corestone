# 15 -- Parked from the teaching page

Written 2026-09-23. Holds material removed from
`GeomorphOnline.github.io/_pages/exercises/corestone-weathering.md` because it
could not be stood behind, not because it is wrong. Nothing here is settled;
it is parked so the page can be honest without the work being lost.

## 1. The timescale comparison

**Why it came off.** Three separate problems, any one of which would do:

1. **The target is unsourced.** "Panola 7, Davis Run 4, Rio Icacos 43-45
   m/Myr" is cited to White et al. (2001), which no route reached -- see design
   14 section 6 for what was tried. We do not know which quantity those numbers
   are: regolith production, front advance, total denudation, mineral-specific
   depletion and catchment solute flux are all reported in m/Myr and are not
   interchangeable.
2. **The model does not compute a front.** Design 13: at t90, 91 % of the
   section is part-reacted at 1.5, 3 and 6 m alike, and `D/t90` rises with the
   depth drawn. The joint network dissolves at every depth at once.
3. **The comparison is not like-for-like.** Design 14: the fast field sites
   have a rindlet zone fracturing at ~2.6 cm generated by the weathering
   itself, against our 1 m inherited joints.

**What it said**, kept verbatim so the argument is recoverable:

> ### Does it get the timescale right?
>
> Nothing in the chemistry or the flow is fitted, so how long weathering takes
> is a *result*. At the default settings -- 1 m joints, 0.30 m/yr, 12 C -- the
> model reacts 90 % of a 3 m section in about **15 000 kyr**. Divide the depth
> by the time and you have something to compare against the field:
> 0.20 m/Myr.
>
> | site | rate | climate |
> |---|---|---|
> | Panola, Georgia | 7 m/Myr | temperate |
> | Davis Run, Virginia | 4 m/Myr | temperate |
> | Rio Icacos, Puerto Rico | 43-45 m/Myr | tropical |
>
> So the model is more than twenty times slower than the temperate pair. [...]
> **The rock that weathers fastest is not jointed a metre apart.** At Rio
> Icacos the granite at the weathering front splits into layers about 2.6 cm
> thick, and the splitting is made by the weathering itself rather than
> inherited from tectonics. [...]
>
> Two cautions come with the comparison, and both are worth more than the
> number. Dividing a depth by a time measures a front only if there *is* a
> front [...] And the field rates are not all measuring the same quantity
> [...] Before comparing two rates, find out what each one is the rate *of*.

**Worth keeping from it**, if any of this returns: the fracture-spacing point
is the strongest thing on the page's own subject, and the two cautions are
transferable well beyond this exercise. What they need is a target that is
sourced and commensurable.

## 2. The intact-granite conductivity claim

The page said the conductivity used to be 1e-8 m/s, *"between twelve and a
hundred times too high"*. Design 14 undercut that from the other side: the
current 5e-10 m/s is **not** laboratory-intact granite -- Stanek & Geraud put
fresh granite at 9.8e-13 to 9.8e-12 m/s, two orders LOWER -- because
Goodfellow sampled granodiorite in a weathering profile. So 5e-10 already sits
in their *altered* class, and whether 1e-8 was "too high" depends entirely on
which rock is meant. The sentence kept its second half, that the flow field
used not to evolve with weathering at all, which is true and is the point.

## 3. Stale numbers, and which kind go stale

Design 12 slowed the model about fourfold, and every CHEMISTRY-dependent
number in the page's control guide went with it:

| claim | was | re-measured 2026-09-23 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 m/yr, 0 C at 2000 kyr | a fourteenth | a twenty-third |
| ...at 5000 kyr | a sixth | an eleventh |
| default at 15 000 kyr | "dissolves completely" | 0.900 |

Those were cut rather than corrected, because they will go stale again on the
next change to the chemistry and they were teaching nothing the surrounding
sentences did not.

**The FLOW and GEOMETRY numbers did not move**, and this is the useful
distinction: the no-joints infiltration fraction re-measures at 0.0420,
exactly the "about a twenty-fourth" the page claims, because it depends on
conductivity and rainfall and not on the reaction. Numbers of that kind can
stay on a page; numbers downstream of the chemistry should not.
